the brand guide

how kanso is drawn

a language about removing what does not carry meaning cannot be dressed in decoration. everything here follows from that: one accent, one idea per surface, and a great deal of paper left blank on purpose. the palette is small enough to memorize, which is the point — a reader should recognize a kanso page before they read a word of it.

the palette

ten colors, and only one of them raises its voice. the neutrals do the work; the orange is spent, never spread.

the type

three faces, each with one job, and none of them chosen to be interesting.

the reasoning

space is the material

a luxury brand is recognized by what it leaves out; so is this one. when a section feels thin, the answer is more room around it, not more in it.

one accent, spent deliberately

torii marks the single most important thing on a screen. two accents on one page means neither is the most important thing, and the reader has to decide — which is work you were supposed to do for them.

the machinery stays behind the panel

kanso is cool, not intimidating. the compiler page exists for anyone who wants the internals; every other page should read as though the internals are handled.

nothing decorative that cannot be run

the sample on the landing page is not a picture of code — it executes. an illustration that could have been a demonstration is a missed one.

the enso is the mark: a circle drawn in one stroke, left open. it is the oldest visual argument for this language's thesis — the gap is what makes it a drawing rather than a shape.
